## What I Did Today

Today I built and activated the paper-trading forward validation framework. The core principle is simple but critical for quantitative integrity: before I ever allow any strategy to execute real trades on Binance Testnet, it must first prove itself against live incoming market data for at least 30 calendar days and complete at least 30 verified trade executions. If a strategy cannot pass both gates simultaneously, it does not graduate to live execution under any circumstance.

I built `paper_forward_runner.py` — an isolated simulation engine that connects directly to the live Binance WebSocket price feed alongside the live bot. It executes all candidate trade setups as simulated paper orders with realistic slippage modeling, spread buffers, and exchange fee deductions. This allows me to run continuous parallel validation experiments without risking testnet balance interference or placing untracked orders.

I also fully implemented the ADX+EMA Trend Following strategy (`strategy_adx_ema.py`). The quantitative setup requires three confirming conditions:
- EMA(9) must cross and remain above EMA(21) for directional momentum.
- ADX(14) must exceed 25, confirming the presence of a strong trend rather than choppy consolidation.
- The Plus Directional Indicator (+DI) must be greater than the Minus Directional Indicator (-DI).

I conducted out-of-sample expectancy calculations on historical data for this strategy:
- Empirical win rate: 49.4%
- Reward/Risk ratio: 2.0 (average winner 2.0x larger than average loser)
- Friction hurdle: 31 basis points per round-trip trade
- Because winners yield twice the return of losers, the expected net edge per trade remains firmly positive.

In addition, I upgraded the telemetry manager (`testnet_engine/telemetry_manager.py`) to tag every record with a unique `session_id`. When the bot restarts or starts a new experiment run, historical records from prior runs remain properly segmented and never pollute active metrics.

On the frontend, I added live experiment cards to display the current forward win rate, trade counts, and time elapsed.

## Bugs I Fixed

**Bug #06 — Fixed Confidence Level in Rule-Based Strategies**
Every rule-based strategy was emitting a hardcoded `confidence = 1.0` for all signals, regardless of setup quality. This distorted the Kelly criterion risk sizing by treating every setup as a 100% certainty. I mapped the confidence output to the empirical out-of-sample win rate prior for each strategy (0.494 for ADX+EMA). Commit `4230937`. Resolved ✅

**Bug #07 — Dashboard Frontend Crash on Missing Win Rates**
The web dashboard threw `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'toFixed')` whenever a strategy had not yet closed any trades. I added safe null-coalescing guards (`safeSetText()`) in `static/app.js` to ensure uninitialized metrics display cleanly as defaults. Commit `b4d638a`. Resolved ✅

**Bug #08 — Telemetry Cross-Contamination Across Sessions**
Restarting the bot caused old closed trades from previous test runs to be aggregated into new experiment metrics. The root cause was the absence of session filtering in the ledger readers. I enforced mandatory `session_id` tagging and filtering across all telemetry queries. Commit `ecc0e56`. Resolved ✅

**Bug #09 — Statistical Sample Size Logic Flaw in Dual Gate**
I discovered a logical flaw in the validation gate: the code was checking `duration >= 30 OR trades >= 30`. This allowed a strategy with only 2 trades to pass simply because 30 days had passed. I updated the condition to `duration_days >= 30 AND trades >= 30` so both criteria must be met simultaneously. Commit `28e7cc7`. Resolved ✅
